# Late-imperial municipal and burgher administration

Read when researching towns, market towns, burgher communities, or their relationship to volosts in the late imperial period. These are research leads, not settlement-specific evidence.

## Historical administrative research reminder

- When researching late-imperial towns, market towns (містечка), burgher communities, or their relationship to volosts, consider the law of **29 April 1875 Old Style (11 May New Style)** extending the 1870 Municipal Statute to western provinces, including Podolia: _О применении Городового положения 16 июня 1870 года к городам Западных губерний_, ПСЗРИ, second collection, vol. 50, no. 54640, pp. 462–464. This is a research lead, not evidence that any particular settlement changed administration on that date.
- The contemporary account in _Энциклопедический словарь Брокгауза и Ефрона_, vol. XX (1897), pp. 331–332, entry [«Местечки»](https://ru.wikisource.org/wiki/ЭСБЕ/Местечки), describes separate burgher communities (where >=10 burgher households) and attachment of smaller groups to another town's burgher community or volost. Distinguish burgher estate administration from territorial incorporation, loss of town status, or volost boundary changes. Do not describe this as a universal law merging towns into neighboring volosts. Verify provisions and settlement-specific implementation before applying claims.
