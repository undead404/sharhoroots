# Search services and human search handoff

Use these services when their coverage fits the research question. Search applicable databases directly rather than relying only on search-engine indexing of their public pages. This is routing guidance, not a requirement to search every service on every task. Preserve the territorial scope, language rules, local-source checks and Nysh discovery step in `AGENTS.md`. Person indexes support an explicit person/surname question or a person already relevant to the investigation; do not turn general settlement research into an unsolicited surname search.

## Choose a service

For specialist routes beyond these six services, start with the triggers in [Specialist historical databases](specialist-databases.md), then read only the relevant sections. That registry adds newspapers, Polish publications, maps, Jewish community history, Holocaust records, military records, repression, bibliography and inventory routes; the access and human-search workflow below applies to them too.

## Verify access and record coverage

Submit a relevant query and observe results, a scoped zero, or a concrete gate. Opening a homepage or filling a form alone does not demonstrate working search. Distinguish search counts, public previews, full entries, source links and access to the underlying document. A failure in a web retrieval tool does not imply that browser search is blocked; use an available browser within the bounded access workflow. Prefer supported connectors or documented APIs when they can perform the search adequately.

Record the service, search date, exact terms and spelling variants, collection, filters, matching/fuzziness settings, displayed counts, pages inspected, caps and gates in the existing task or returned evidence record. Save stable result/record links and full archival references. Do not store transient access observations as historical evidence in `content/`. Reuse accumulated coverage on resumption; search again when the question, coverage or access materially changes.

Search results are leads. A catalogue entry can establish what the catalogue describes; it does not establish the uninspected case's contents. Keep machine-generated indexes and OCR/HTR distinct from user-confirmed readings. The prohibition on LLM reading of archival images still applies after login: use index text, permitted local OCR/HTR and specific human readings, not browser screenshots of scans for model transcription.

## Ask the user to run a search

When a relevant gate prevents retrieval and the user's access could materially advance the question, request a focused search even if no specific case has yet been identified. Offer the choice of logging in within the agent-accessible browser or returning results from the user's own session. Do not ask for passwords or repeated login when an authenticated session already works.

Include:

- The service and direct search/collection link, plus the question the search could resolve.
- Exact terms and justified historical spelling variants, filters, date range and matching mode. Distinguish alternative searches from criteria to combine.
- The useful output: relevant rows with descriptions, full archival references, dates, collection names and stable record/source links. Request pasted text or a supported export; ask only for the needed subset.
- Coverage: displayed count, pages inspected, any cap or truncation, and the exact query/settings for a zero. A zero is bounded by that database's indexed coverage and the query used.

For example: «У пошуку фонду 442 перевірте “Шаргород” і “Шаргородъ” окремо, зазначте режим точного написання й кількість збігів. Для справ про [питання] надішліть назву, повний шифр, роки та посилання; якщо сторінок кілька, зазначте, які переглянуто».

Batch related requests and continue independent research. Keep an essential unanswered request pending in the task checkpoint; use `blocked` only when that dependency prevents further progress. On receipt, record the query and user-supplied results with their stated scope and qualifications, follow useful document links, and resume the investigation. Do not ask for corroboration merely because the result was supplied by the user; do not silently promote machine text into a confirmed reading.
