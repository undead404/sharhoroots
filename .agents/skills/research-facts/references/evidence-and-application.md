# Evaluate findings and apply evidence

Read when evaluating candidate findings, drawing inferences (including negative evidence), drafting an answer, or applying findings to articles. Follow the numbered steps after the search procedure.

10. **Build a small evidence record.** For each candidate fact, capture:
    - the proposed claim;
    - the evidence and a precise locator;
    - source type (primary/secondary) and what it can establish;
    - caveats, conflicting evidence, and confidence in the reading separately from confidence in the historical assertion;
    - for living or recently deceased people, the privacy decision and any details omitted, generalized, or withheld, with a concise reason rather than sensitive data.

    Accept user-confirmed readings without requiring independent rereading. Record what the document asserts and what that assertion can establish separately: a perfectly transcribed administrative report may still contain incorrect names or casualty figures. Assess its purpose, proximity to the event, basis of knowledge, and material conflicts. When only the report's assertion is supported, write «У звіті зазначено…» rather than presenting its contents as an independently established event. Do not invent doubt or demand corroboration merely because the reading came from the user; retain known source limits and uncertainty.

    Prefer well-supported, locally relevant findings from research, but do not treat named people as trivia or require fame/notability. Preserve each person's source-supported local role, action, or appearance in the appropriate article, including one-time and otherwise obscure mentions, subject to the root contract's privacy assessment. Seek independent corroboration for consequential or surprising research findings when feasible; accepting a user-confirmed reading does not settle the reliability of the underlying assertion.

11. **Apply a reader-value gate without discarding people.** For ordinary findings, name the reader-facing question a detail answers and keep search exclusions, low-value negatives, and research-process narration in the task. A named person in a source relevant to the target territory does not need to be famous or independently notable to merit inclusion: preserve their source-supported name and local connection in the appropriate article, even for a single appearance. A role seated in a target settlement can support a qualified inference of probable presence there (for example, a volost chief appointed to a seat in Lozova); explain the evidence and label the inference rather than presenting it as an explicit visit. A person whose only known role is external (for example, a judge in Mogilev) is not locally relevant on that basis alone; include them only when their specific action materially documents the target territory or its inhabitants. Do not add a sentence merely to document that a place, person, or event is absent from an unrelated list.
12. **Treat negative evidence narrowly.** An omission can establish that a name does not appear in a particular inspected list; it does not by itself establish that an event did not happen, that an entity remained somewhere, or that its status and boundaries were unchanged. Before using an omission as evidence, verify that the source is an exhaustive list for the specific action, place, period, and administrative level; that the entity's identity and relevant name/status are stable and match the list; and that plausible renaming, regrouping, or other explanations do not defeat the inference. If those conditions are uncertain, report only the source-bounded observation (if it is reader-useful) and keep the inference in the task. Even a valid negative inference belongs in an article only when it materially answers that article's question; evidential validity alone is not editorial relevance.
13. **Write for `content/` in Ukrainian.** Follow the repository's language rule without exception for requests to write in another language: all authored text, including descriptions and headings, must be Ukrainian. The only other-language text allowed is the exact title of an actual publication. Translate or summarize quotations and other source wording; render foreign personal and place names in Ukrainian script. Follow nearby notes' organization and the `citations-and-notes` skill for Markdown source footnotes, put references next to the claims they support, separate source wording from editorial interpretation, and do not embellish a claim for dramatic effect. Preserve existing frontmatter structure, wikilinks, embeds, and source links.
14. **Apply task findings to their targets.** For a research task, save verified findings and source details in the task first and set `status: found`. Then assess each target article separately: apply relevant, supported findings, preserving the note's structure, citations, and uncertainty; add Obsidian wikilinks to relevant existing notes rather than leaving cross-linking to the user. Preserve source-supported people and their local roles/actions/appearances; do not omit them through the ordinary reader-value gate. For other findings, record in the task why supported details were not included when they fail that gate. When drafting or revising article prose, bold the article's title at its natural first mention. Set `status: done` after all supported local-person mentions and other article-worthy findings are applied and the task records the disposition of remaining findings. If bounded research yields no new supported information or useful inference and no essential source remains pending, record the attempts and limits, leave target articles unchanged unless a supported qualification or local-person mention is warranted, and set `status: no_result`. Do not use `no_result` for interrupted work or to imply the fact/document does not exist. If work stops because of a technical failure that prevents continuation, record the concrete error and set `status: fail`; use `blocked` instead when a specific essential source/access/response is unavailable. Reopen `no_result` only for a materially different or newly available research route, and retry `fail` after addressing or working around its technical cause.
    Treat `targets` as provisional routing metadata. Before applying findings or closing a task, search `content/` using the findings' subjects, historical names, aliases, and relevant links; inspect candidate articles and add or correct the paths of existing articles that the findings genuinely inform. Reassess destinations when new connections emerge. An empty list does not establish that there is no suitable article or no reader value. For each supported local-person mention and other article-worthy fact or useful qualified inference, record its disposition in `## Докази й відповідь`: the article path and section where it was applied or already appears, or a specific editorial reason for exclusion. Verify application or existing coverage against the actual article text and review the content diff before marking `done`. Do not close by iterating over an empty target list or by giving a blanket low-value dismissal. If a valuable finding has no suitable existing article, retain `found` and record the unresolved destination; propose the needed article or placement without creating a new article unless authorized. Useful qualified inferences count as findings even when the original question remains partly unanswered.
    For a standalone request to find facts, do not edit article files unless asked: return the evidence record and suggested Ukrainian wording. State what remains unverified and which sources you actually inspected. Following a research task through `start-task` includes applying findings to relevant existing articles discovered during target assessment; it does not authorize creating new articles.
