# Historical search procedure

Read before selecting searches for a new investigation or a substantive coverage gap. Reuse completed coverage on continuation.

Before selecting external searches, read the trigger/priority section of the [specialist database registry](specialist-databases.md), then only its applicable resource sections. For archival case discovery or relevant person-index searches, also read [Search services and human search handoff](search-services.md). Use applicable services alongside web search; choose the highest-value route for the specific question, follow original references, and add another route only for a material coverage gap, different source corpus or access need. Reuse recorded coverage and check current browser access rather than treating a past retrieval error as permanent inaccessibility. Apply the registry's place-name/jurisdiction fallback and deduplicate underlying sources, not merely result URLs.

When selecting destinations, load and follow `../../content-placement/SKILL.md`. For each article-worthy finding, identify the historical subject, primary existing page and section, useful cross-references, and any justified extraction proposal; check that routing implies no unsupported institutional continuity. Record placement in the existing evidence/disposition record, without adding a new task template or treating a registrar as the home for the population it served.

1. **Define the question.** Identify the place, period, people or institution, and what would count as an interesting answer. Ask a concise question if the scope is too broad to research responsibly.
2. **Check the task queue.** Read relevant notes in `tasks/` before starting. Do not duplicate an open task; if the request spans independently answerable questions, keep the current answer focused and capture the other questions as separate tasks.
3. **Check reusable sources before web searching.** Look through `sources/` for source records relevant to the question, then check the corresponding local copies under `books/`. A source record is a lead and retrieval aid, not evidence; verify the source itself and its relevant passage. Reuse retained passage text and established user readings with their recorded provenance and qualifications; do not redownload or rerun recognition merely because the investigation resumes. Recheck applicability (edition, date, place, units, population and meaning), and inspect surrounding text when the new claim extends beyond the recorded passage. Cross-source conclusions and recurring contextual findings remain in their originating task under «Докази й відповідь», with searchable terminology, scope, source locators and links from later tasks; source-specific assessments belong in the existing source record. Do not promote an old summary or unverified OCR into evidence. A previously downloaded copy may satisfy the requirement to process a source document, but record what you actually inspected and its precise locator. Do not download another copy when a usable local copy is already present.
4. **Search the web and inspect documents.** A new investigation must include web searching and processing of at least one source document (for example, a page of a book, archival image, map, official text, or a relevant local document), following the archival-reading restrictions in AGENTS.md. Coverage accumulates in the research record across resumptions and delegated work; do not restart completed language searches or source inspection for each session. A reuse/application-only continuation needs no fresh search unless a new claim, conflict, coverage gap or changed access creates a substantive reason. For research concerning the territory of former Sharhorod raion, always search in Ukrainian, then route additional languages by the historical period and subject:
   - **Russian:** research about periods before 1991, and any topic concerning Old Believers.
   - **Lithuanian:** research about 1363–1596.
   - **Turkish:** research about 1672–1699.
   - **Polish:** research about periods before 1930, whenever Polish people, institutions, or topics are relevant, and for every Roman Catholic topic regardless of period.
   - **Hebrew and Yiddish:** research concerning Jews or Judaism, and any topic concerning urban life. Search in both languages when this rule applies.
   - **German:** research about periods 1941–1944, and any topic concerning Austrian subjects or Lutheranism.
   - **Romanian:** research about the period 1941–1944.

   This language list is not exhaustive. Infer additional search languages from explicit context such as a person's stated citizenship or origin, an institution, or a relevant source tradition. Do not infer a person's origin from their name alone. These rules determine search coverage, not which credible, locally relevant findings may be used.

   Apply period cutoffs to the period being researched, not to the publication date of a source. Adapt each query to the language rather than translating literally; use historically appropriate place names, spellings, administrative terms, and relevant scripts (including Cyrillic and Latin forms, and Hebrew script where appropriate). Do not guess spellings or treat a translated name as the only form. Record the exact queries, the language and reason for using each, and useful results in the task or returned research record. A search result is a lead, not a processed document. Record each inspected document with its title/creator, date, exact page/folio/image or other locator, URL or shelfmark, and what it can establish. Prefer primary sources for claims about events, with reputable secondary sources for context. A catalog description establishes what the catalog says, not the uninspected file's contents.

5. **Use a bounded access workflow.** For each relevant blocked resource, try no more than two materially different automated retrieval methods; do not repeat the same failing request or keep probing a site. A web-tool failure or a readable homepage does not establish whether interactive search works: when available, use the browser as a distinct method and verify submitted results. Reuse the user's authenticated session. At a login wall, ask the user to sign in or supply results when useful; do not bypass login requirements, CAPTCHAs, paywalls, or explicit access restrictions. Put temporary downloads and command output in ignored `.agent-work/`; put an intentionally retained source copy in ignored `books/` and add or update a reusable record in `sources/` when it is likely to help future research. Record meaningful access failures briefly in the task, not in article prose.
6. **Request useful human searches or retrieval.** Ask the user to operate a gated catalogue or index when its coverage could materially advance the question and the agent cannot retrieve the needed results. A specific document need not already be identified. Follow the handoff format in [search-services.md](search-services.md): site/link, research purpose, exact query variants, filters and matching modes, and the result rows, references, links and coverage needed. Batch related requests and continue independent work while waiting. For an already identified blocked document, request only the relevant pages/folios or excerpt when available alternatives are inadequate; ask that a retained digital copy be placed in `books/` when appropriate. Do not ask for every blocked link. While an essential response or access path is pending and no next research step can proceed, set the task to `blocked`; otherwise continue as `in_progress` and record the pending request. Resume blocked work when the dependency becomes available. Do not use `no_result` while an essential response is pending.
7. **Save worthwhile follow-up leads.** If research uncovers a distinct, potentially useful question that is not answered in the current scope, load and follow `create-research-task` to create its note. Supply the lead/evidence that prompted it, a focused question, useful search terms or source leads, preserved uncertainty, and every article path the answer would inform. Capture every material follow-up lead; do not turn trivial tangents into queue noise. Keep each independently answerable question separate; article count does not determine task scope.
8. **Inspect and locate the evidence.** Verify the relevant passage, page, image, folio, entry, or map location in the underlying source where possible. Preserve original spelling in research records outside `content/` when needed to identify or verify evidence. In `content/`, translate or summarize quotations and source wording in Ukrainian; preserve another language only for the exact title of an actual publication. Render foreign personal and place names in Ukrainian script, using established Ukrainian forms or consistent transliteration/transcription without adding the original spelling alongside them. If web search or document access is unavailable, record that in the task or returned research record; do not imply that the minimum was met or put the access failure into article prose.
9. **Be explicit about limits in the right place.** In article prose, include only a concise qualification needed to accurately interpret the historical claim (for example, that a catalog entry establishes what the catalog says, not the contents of an uninspected file). Do not narrate the research process or the agent's environment in an article: failed access attempts, HTTP/tool errors, unavailable tools, and similar operational details belong in the task's research log and evidence/answer sections. Never mark incomplete evidence as `found` or `done`. Do not turn an access failure into article prose merely to disclose that the research was incomplete.
