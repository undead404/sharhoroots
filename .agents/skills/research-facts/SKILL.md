# Research sourced local-history facts

Load and follow `../manage-sources/SKILL.md` when finding or maintaining reusable source records, registering local copies or retained texts, or recording inspected passages. That skill owns the source schema and storage contract; keep research queries, access attempts, interpretations, and article application in this workflow's task/evidence record. Existing source metadata is a retrieval aid, not a substitute for inspecting the underlying evidence.

When creating or revising quotations, source citations, footnotes, or author/editor notes, load and follow `../citations-and-notes/SKILL.md`. This includes paraphrasing evidence and moving cited passages; stay within this workflow’s editing scope.

Help the user discover interesting facts without turning plausible stories, search snippets, or weak citations into asserted history. “Proved” means supported by evidence that readers can inspect; do not imply absolute certainty where the sources do not provide it.

This evidence workflow governs claims found in outside sources and the assistant's own inferences. Treat facts the user states—including their own source readings or transcriptions—as established unless they mark uncertainty or explicitly ask for verification. Do not demand corroboration or weaken those facts because outside evidence is absent; if inspected sources conflict, report the difference without silently replacing the user's account.

## Research effort and continuity

Optimize useful, supported findings per total agent and quota usage (across OpenCode, Codex, or Antigravity), not elapsed time. Delegation is not inherently cheaper; include briefing, inherited instructions/context, the worker's research, returned material, integration and verification. Do not claim quota savings from shorter elapsed time or fewer visible messages.

- **Retrieve selectively.** Start with filename/heading searches and relevant task checkpoints, then read matching evidence sections and source passages. Use exact locators to extract the needed pages or text windows; expand when context, contradictions or completeness require it. Avoid dumping whole queues, books, OCR runs or long logs into context. Batch independent lookups, but return only useful results. Load specialist workflows when their operation is needed, not every skill mentioned in a research log.
- **Choose the next unit of work.** Reuse an applicable established result first; do a short, tightly coupled lookup directly. Investigate an unexpected lead now when it changes the answer, resolves a consequential conflict, identifies a locally relevant person or unlocks essential evidence. Preserve worthwhile independent questions as linked atomic tasks under step 7; postpone low-value tangents without creating queue noise. Do not use a fixed search/source count as a stopping rule: continue while a concrete route could materially improve the answer, or record the specific dependency that prevents it.
- **Delegate selectively when available and permitted.** A subagent is appropriate for an independently answerable, necessary question with a clear deliverable when substantial exploratory reading would otherwise accumulate in a parent investigation that still has work to do. Keep short lookups, tightly coupled interpretation and final synthesis direct. Prefer direct work when delegation would copy most of the parent context, duplicate searches, or require similarly extensive rereading. Context isolation can help but is not evidence of lower total usage. Use a minimal brief rather than full history when supported: question and relevance, existing findings and exhausted routes, exact file/source pointers, applicable repository rules, allowed operations, evidence standard and completion condition. Do not send archival images to a model. Assign separate questions or source ranges and one owner for each edited file; avoid recursive delegation or duplicate independent passes unless their evidential value justifies the extra usage.
- **Return evidence, not a second transcript.** A delegated investigation returns its answer or specific blocker, precise supporting locators and retained-text paths, source/coverage limits, conflicts, useful searches and next unresolved step. Save durable evidence in the existing task/source mechanisms; the parent integrates the result and checks the actual supporting passages, not the worker's authority. Do not automatically repeat the worker's entire search. Delegation itself does not justify opening a new task or creating a new storage layer.
- **Checkpoint at meaningful boundaries.** Before a handoff, context compaction, pause or change of research direction, update the concise current-state block near the top of the task (see tasks/TEMPLATE.md). Point to evidence and retained readings rather than copying them; preserve conflicts, unresolved questions, coverage, exhausted routes and the next useful action. Keep historical logs and raw readings intact below or at their existing linked paths; explicitly supersede stale blockers. On resumption, read this block and relevant evidence first, expanding into the log only as needed. Operational sharing failures remain separate from historical blockers.
- **Defer a timed retry.** When an essential dependency is temporarily unavailable and there is a concrete retry time, use `status: deferred` with a quoted ISO 8601 `not_before` including the date and UTC offset; record the timing basis and any buffer. Before that instant do not probe the resource. Once due, `start-task` treats the task as first priority alongside `in_progress` and checks access; a due time is not proof of restored access. On success use `in_progress` and remove the time gate; on failure either set a justified later retry or use `blocked` if no retry time is known. This is queue eligibility, not an automatic launch. Follow `.opencode/commands/start-task.md` for selection and retry details.

For example, comparing the Murafa commission's two allowance amounts calls first for checking the recorded currency evidence and the exact nominal relationship. Purchasing power in a particular place/month is a different question; pursue it only if it materially informs interpretation. A substantial necessary currency investigation can be isolated; a single applicable source check normally stays direct. Neither route may turn nominal equivalence into market equivalence or reconcile conflicting event dates by assumption.

## Workflow

Before selecting external searches, read the trigger/priority section of the [specialist database registry](references/specialist-databases.md), then only its applicable resource sections. For archival case discovery or relevant person-index searches, also read [Search services and human search handoff](references/search-services.md). Use applicable services alongside web search; choose the highest-value route for the specific question, follow original references, and add another route only for a material coverage gap, different source corpus or access need. Reuse recorded coverage and check current browser access rather than treating a past retrieval error as permanent inaccessibility. Apply the registry's place-name/jurisdiction fallback and deduplicate underlying sources, not merely result URLs.

When selecting destinations, load and follow `../content-placement/SKILL.md`. For each article-worthy finding, identify the historical subject, primary existing page and section, useful cross-references, and any justified extraction proposal; check that routing implies no unsupported institutional continuity. Record placement in the existing evidence/disposition record, without adding a new task template or treating a registrar as the home for the population it served.

1. **Define the question.** Identify the place, period, people or institution, and what would count as an interesting answer. Ask a concise question if the scope is too broad to research responsibly.
2. **Check the task queue.** Read relevant notes in `tasks/` before starting. Do not duplicate an open task; if the request spans independently answerable questions, keep the current answer focused and capture the other questions as separate tasks.
3. **Check reusable sources before web searching.** Look through `sources/` for source records relevant to the question, then check the corresponding local copies under `books/`. A source record is a lead and retrieval aid, not evidence; verify the source itself and its relevant passage. Reuse retained passage text and established user readings with their recorded provenance and qualifications; do not redownload or rerun recognition merely because the investigation resumes. Recheck applicability (edition, date, place, units, population and meaning), and inspect surrounding text when the new claim extends beyond the recorded passage. Cross-source conclusions and recurring contextual findings remain in their originating task under «Докази й відповідь», with searchable terminology, scope, source locators and links from later tasks; source-specific assessments belong in the existing source record. Do not promote an old summary or unverified OCR into evidence. A previously downloaded copy may satisfy the requirement to process a source document, but record what you actually inspected and its precise locator. Do not download another copy when a usable local copy is already present.
4. **Search the web and inspect documents.** A new investigation must include web searching and processing of at least one source document (for example, a page of a book, archival image, map, official text, or a relevant local document), following the archival-reading restrictions in AGENTS.md. Coverage accumulates in the research record across resumptions and delegated work; do not restart completed language searches or source inspection for each session. A reuse/application-only continuation needs no fresh search unless a new claim, conflict, coverage gap or changed access creates a substantive reason. For research concerning the territory of former Sharhorod raion, always search in Ukrainian, then route additional languages by the historical period and subject:
   - **Russian:** research about periods before 1991, and any topic concerning Old Believers.
   - **Lithuanian:** research about 1363–1596.
   - **Turkish:** research about 1672–1699.
   - **Polish:** research about periods before 1930, whenever Polish people, institutions, or topics are relevant, and for every Roman Catholic topic regardless of period.
   - **Hebrew and Yiddish:** research concerning Jews or Judaism, and any topic concerning urban life. Search in both languages when this rule applies.
   - **German:** research about periods 1941–1944, and any topic concerning Austrian subjects or Lutheranism.
   - **Romanian:** research about the period 1941–1944.

   This language list is not exhaustive. Infer additional search languages from explicit context such as a person's stated citizenship or origin, an institution, or a relevant source tradition. Do not infer a person's origin from their name alone. These rules determine search coverage, not which credible, locally relevant findings may be used.

   Apply period cutoffs to the period being researched, not to the publication date of a source. Adapt each query to the language rather than translating literally; use historically appropriate place names, spellings, administrative terms, and relevant scripts (including Cyrillic and Latin forms, and Hebrew script where appropriate). Do not guess spellings or treat a translated name as the only form. Record the exact queries, the language and reason for using each, and useful results in the task or returned research record. A search result is a lead, not a processed document. Record each inspected document with its title/creator, date, exact page/folio/image or other locator, URL or shelfmark, and what it can establish. Prefer primary sources for claims about events, with reputable secondary sources for context. A catalog description establishes what the catalog says, not the uninspected file's contents.

5. **Use a bounded access workflow.** For each relevant blocked resource, try no more than two materially different automated retrieval methods; do not repeat the same failing request or keep probing a site. A web-tool failure or a readable homepage does not establish whether interactive search works: when available, use the browser as a distinct method and verify submitted results. Reuse the user's authenticated session. At a login wall, ask the user to sign in or supply results when useful; do not bypass login requirements, CAPTCHAs, paywalls, or explicit access restrictions. Put temporary downloads and command output in ignored `.agent-work/`; put an intentionally retained source copy in ignored `books/` and add or update a reusable record in `sources/` when it is likely to help future research. Record meaningful access failures briefly in the task, not in article prose.
6. **Request useful human searches or retrieval.** Ask the user to operate a gated catalogue or index when its coverage could materially advance the question and the agent cannot retrieve the needed results. A specific document need not already be identified. Follow the handoff format in [search-services.md](references/search-services.md): site/link, research purpose, exact query variants, filters and matching modes, and the result rows, references, links and coverage needed. Batch related requests and continue independent work while waiting. For an already identified blocked document, request only the relevant pages/folios or excerpt when available alternatives are inadequate; ask that a retained digital copy be placed in `books/` when appropriate. Do not ask for every blocked link. While an essential response or access path is pending and no next research step can proceed, set the task to `blocked`; otherwise continue as `in_progress` and record the pending request. Resume blocked work when the dependency becomes available. Do not use `no_result` while an essential response is pending.
7. **Save worthwhile follow-up leads.** If research uncovers a distinct, potentially useful question that is not answered in the current scope, load and follow `create-research-task` to create its note. Supply the lead/evidence that prompted it, a focused question, useful search terms or source leads, preserved uncertainty, and every article path the answer would inform. Capture every material follow-up lead; do not turn trivial tangents into queue noise. Keep each independently answerable question separate; article count does not determine task scope.
8. **Inspect and locate the evidence.** Verify the relevant passage, page, image, folio, entry, or map location in the underlying source where possible. Preserve original spelling in research records outside `content/` when needed to identify or verify evidence. In `content/`, translate or summarize quotations and source wording in Ukrainian; preserve another language only for the exact title of an actual publication. Render foreign personal and place names in Ukrainian script, using established Ukrainian forms or consistent transliteration/transcription without adding the original spelling alongside them. If web search or document access is unavailable, record that in the task or returned research record; do not imply that the minimum was met or put the access failure into article prose.
9. **Be explicit about limits in the right place.** In article prose, include only a concise qualification needed to accurately interpret the historical claim (for example, that a catalog entry establishes what the catalog says, not the contents of an uninspected file). Do not narrate the research process or the agent's environment in an article: failed access attempts, HTTP/tool errors, unavailable tools, and similar operational details belong in the task's research log and evidence/answer sections. Never mark incomplete evidence as `found` or `done`. Do not turn an access failure into article prose merely to disclose that the research was incomplete.
10. **Build a small evidence record.** For each candidate fact, capture:
    - the proposed claim;
    - the evidence and a precise locator;
    - source type (primary/secondary) and what it can establish;
    - caveats, conflicting evidence, and confidence.

    Prefer well-supported, locally relevant findings from research, but do not treat named people as trivia or require fame/notability. Preserve each person's source-supported local role, action, or appearance in the appropriate article, including one-time and otherwise obscure mentions. Seek independent corroboration for consequential or surprising research findings when feasible; do not apply this as a requirement to user-provided facts.

11. **Apply a reader-value gate without discarding people.** For ordinary findings, name the reader-facing question a detail answers and keep search exclusions, low-value negatives, and research-process narration in the task. A named person in a source relevant to the target territory does not need to be famous or independently notable to merit inclusion: preserve their source-supported name and local connection in the appropriate article, even for a single appearance. A role seated in a target settlement can support a qualified inference of probable presence there (for example, a volost chief appointed to a seat in Lozova); explain the evidence and label the inference rather than presenting it as an explicit visit. A person whose only known role is external (for example, a judge in Mogilev) is not locally relevant on that basis alone; include them only when their specific action materially documents the target territory or its inhabitants. Do not add a sentence merely to document that a place, person, or event is absent from an unrelated list.
12. **Treat negative evidence narrowly.** An omission can establish that a name does not appear in a particular inspected list; it does not by itself establish that an event did not happen, that an entity remained somewhere, or that its status and boundaries were unchanged. Before using an omission as evidence, verify that the source is an exhaustive list for the specific action, place, period, and administrative level; that the entity's identity and relevant name/status are stable and match the list; and that plausible renaming, regrouping, or other explanations do not defeat the inference. If those conditions are uncertain, report only the source-bounded observation (if it is reader-useful) and keep the inference in the task. Even a valid negative inference belongs in an article only when it materially answers that article's question; evidential validity alone is not editorial relevance.
13. **Write for `content/` in Ukrainian.** Follow the repository's language rule without exception for requests to write in another language: all authored text, including descriptions and headings, must be Ukrainian. The only other-language text allowed is the exact title of an actual publication. Translate or summarize quotations and other source wording; render foreign personal and place names in Ukrainian script. Follow nearby notes' organization and the `citations-and-notes` skill for Markdown source footnotes, put references next to the claims they support, separate source wording from editorial interpretation, and do not embellish a claim for dramatic effect. Preserve existing frontmatter structure, wikilinks, embeds, and source links.
14. **Apply task findings to their targets.** For a research task, save verified findings and source details in the task first and set `status: found`. Then assess each target article separately: apply relevant, supported findings, preserving the note's structure, citations, and uncertainty; add Obsidian wikilinks to relevant existing notes rather than leaving cross-linking to the user. Preserve source-supported people and their local roles/actions/appearances; do not omit them through the ordinary reader-value gate. For other findings, record in the task why supported details were not included when they fail that gate. When drafting or revising article prose, bold the article's title at its natural first mention. Set `status: done` after all supported local-person mentions and other article-worthy findings are applied and the task records the disposition of remaining findings. If bounded research yields no new supported information or useful inference and no essential source remains pending, record the attempts and limits, leave target articles unchanged unless a supported qualification or local-person mention is warranted, and set `status: no_result`. Do not use `no_result` for interrupted work or to imply the fact/document does not exist. If work stops because of a technical failure that prevents continuation, record the concrete error and set `status: fail`; use `blocked` instead when a specific essential source/access/response is unavailable. Reopen `no_result` only for a materially different or newly available research route, and retry `fail` after addressing or working around its technical cause.
    Treat `targets` as provisional routing metadata. Before applying findings or closing a task, search `content/` using the findings' subjects, historical names, aliases, and relevant links; inspect candidate articles and add or correct the paths of existing articles that the findings genuinely inform. Reassess destinations when new connections emerge. An empty list does not establish that there is no suitable article or no reader value. For each supported local-person mention and other article-worthy fact or useful qualified inference, record its disposition in `## Докази й відповідь`: the article path and section where it was applied or already appears, or a specific editorial reason for exclusion. Verify application or existing coverage against the actual article text and review the content diff before marking `done`. Do not close by iterating over an empty target list or by giving a blanket low-value dismissal. If a valuable finding has no suitable existing article, retain `found` and record the unresolved destination; propose the needed article or placement without creating a new article unless authorized. Useful qualified inferences count as findings even when the original question remains partly unanswered.
    For a standalone request to find facts, do not edit article files unless asked: return the evidence record and suggested Ukrainian wording. State what remains unverified and which sources you actually inspected. Following a research task through `start-task` includes applying findings to relevant existing articles discovered during target assessment; it does not authorize creating new articles.

## Atomic task convention

- A task is one answerable research question with one completion outcome. It may require several sources and may target multiple articles, but it is not a list of independent questions or edits.
- Use `targets` as a YAML sequence of article paths (not a singular `target` field). List every article the findings are intended to inform and update the list as research reveals relevant existing destinations. An empty sequence records no currently identified target; assess destinations from the findings before concluding that no article is in scope.
- Keep task notes in `tasks/`, based on `tasks/TEMPLATE.md`. Task states are: `queued` before work begins; `in_progress` while research is underway and can proceed; `deferred` for an essential dependency's timed retry with a required `not_before`; `blocked` while a specific essential source/access/response is unavailable without a concrete retry time; `found` when new supported findings and their evidence are saved in the task but article-worthy findings are not yet applied; `done` after article-worthy findings are applied and the task records why other supported findings were kept out of public prose; `no_result` when bounded research found no useful supported information after alternatives were exhausted and no essential source remains pending; and `fail` when a technical failure stopped work, not when research reached a historical conclusion. `no_result` is not evidence that the searched-for fact or document does not exist. Check deferred tasks when due; resume blocked tasks as `in_progress` when the dependency is met; reopen `no_result` only for a materially different or newly available research route; retry `fail` after addressing or working around its technical cause. In the access/pending-response rules above, use `deferred` instead of `blocked` whenever a concrete retry time is known.
- Record web queries and the actual source documents processed in the task. For an ordinary research request without an existing task, include these in the returned evidence record; create task notes only for distinct worthwhile follow-ups.
- Reuse local source copies and recorded search coverage when available. Batch useful human search/retrieval requests under step 6; do not repeat an unanswered request without a substantive change, and do not let nonessential access failures stall the research.
- When applying findings, add Obsidian wikilinks to relevant existing notes and bold the target article's title at its natural first mention. Do not create links to nonexistent notes or add irrelevant links merely to increase link count.

## Completion check

- Target discovery has been checked against the actual findings and existing articles, even if `targets` was initially empty. Each valuable finding has a recorded destination and verified application or existing coverage, or a specific justified exclusion. A valuable finding awaiting a suitable destination keeps the task `found`; an empty target list never makes completion automatic.
- Each historical or genealogical assertion derived from research has a reference that supports that specific assertion. User-provided facts do not require independent corroboration; retain any uncertainty the user marked.
- Each research-derived article finding is locally relevant and supported. User-provided facts are treated as established under the rule above. Named people with a source-supported connection to the target territory are not excluded as low-value or insufficiently notable; external people without such a connection are not made local by their name alone.
- Negative evidence is stated only at the scope the source supports; absence from a list is not turned into proof of non-occurrence, continued status, or unchanged boundaries without an exhaustive, identity-matched comparison and consideration of alternatives.
- The locator allows a reader to find the evidence. Article prose distinguishes what a source establishes from what remains uncertain, without exposing research-process or agent-environment failures; those details are recorded in the task.
- The investigation record includes web search coverage and at least one processed source document, or explicitly records why the work remains incomplete. Resumption and application-only work reuse recorded coverage rather than repeat it. A source record alone is not a processed document.
- A task marked `found` has evidence recorded but supported local-person mentions and other article-worthy findings have not yet been applied; a task marked `done` has those mentions and findings applied and records why other supported findings failing the reader-value gate were kept out of public prose; a task marked `deferred` names the dependency, a timezone-explicit `not_before`, and the retry's timing basis; a task marked `blocked` names the essential dependency and why it matters without a known retry time; a task marked `no_result` records the bounded search, limits, and why no supported finding or useful inference emerged without implying nonexistence; a task marked `fail` records the technical error that stopped work and is not presented as a research result.
- Target article prose added or revised from findings includes appropriate Obsidian wikilinks to relevant existing notes and bolds the article title at its natural first mention.
- Before applying findings, check all new or revised reader-facing text in `content/` for Ukrainian wording and Ukrainian-script rendering of foreign personal and place names; retain another language only in exact publication titles.
- Each worthwhile unanswered follow-up discovered during research has a non-duplicative, atomic task note.
- Names, dates, places, and quoted wording in research records have not been silently normalized or inferred; reader-facing text in `content/` follows the Ukrainian-language and name-rendering rules above.
- The Ukrainian prose does not state more than the evidence supports.
