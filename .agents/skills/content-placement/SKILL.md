# Place content by historical entity

Use this skill when choosing where historical or genealogical information belongs, deciding cross-references, or assessing an extraction or merge. Apply it within the calling workflow's evidence and editing permissions; it does not authorize research, new articles, or reorganization on its own. Paths in the examples below are relative to `content/`.

- **Route by historical subject, not article length or source provenance.** Before applying a finding, identify its subject and period, inspect plausible existing pages, choose the primary home for substantive treatment, and decide which other pages need a distinct perspective or a brief linked mention. A record's keeper does not determine the home of every fact it contains. Existing misplaced material is not a placement precedent.
- **Settlements (`content/settlement/`) cover the whole settlement:** all inhabitants regardless of religion, ethnicity, or social group, and demographic, economic, political, and administrative developments. Their scope runs from inception to dissolution or abandonment when those boundaries are known; otherwise do not impose an arbitrary time limit or infer an endpoint. Preserve existing settlement sections and conventions, including chronological history and specialized record tables; these placement rules require no new sections.
- **Registrars (`content/registrar/`) cover registration functions and the relevant institutions.** Religious registrars cover all pre-secular life-event registration, registers, parish jurisdiction, clergy, religious life, and institutional history. Judaic registrars cover Jewish metrical records, synagogues and religious institutions, kahal administration, and relevant jurisdictional history—not the general history of Jewish inhabitants. Civil registrars cover civil vital records, registration authorities, procedures, and jurisdiction—not the entire administrative history of village councils. Context about other records may help readers find evidence without transferring the population's history to the registrar.
- **Do not turn the site's grouping into historical identity.** A registrar page may bring together records of different periods or bodies. Distinguish the kahal, religious congregation, synagogue, rabbinate, metrical official, or civil authority as the evidence allows; shared place, population, building, filename, or page title does not prove institutional identity, subordination, succession, or uninterrupted continuity. Preserve dated distinctions and leave unsupported relationships unresolved.
- **Extract a distinct entity, not a long section.** Long articles are acceptable. An independent page is warranted when a documented historical entity has a coherent history, functions, relationships, or records worth treating independently. Use existing folders and types: other organizations such as collective farms, factories, and clubs belong under `content/organization/` when sufficient substantive material warrants extraction; a new record genre alone does not warrant a page. Check for an existing entity page first. Propose extraction when useful, but create or split pages only within the workflow's authorized scope, following `curate-articles` and the redirect rules when applicable.
- **Cross-reference without needless duplication.** Keep the substantial account in its primary home; elsewhere give the relevant consequence or concise context with an Obsidian link to that account, preferably its section when useful. Multiple substantive treatments are appropriate when the event matters differently to each entity; preserve evidence and citations for each account's claims. Do not copy the full narrative into every related page or remove valuable personal evidence merely to shorten a page.

Use these existing pages as decision tests, not as proof that all their current material is correctly placed:

- In `settlement/Sharhorod.md`, city population figures and the lives of Jewish residents belong to the city's history; `registrar/Sharhorod-judaic.md` owns rabbis, synagogues, metrical practice, and kahal jurisdiction. A kahal total including attached settlements must not become the city's population total. A resident's dispute with the monastery can inform both city life and the monastery's property history; the resident's religion alone does not route it to the Judaic registrar.
- For `settlement/Murafa.md` and `settlement/Nova-Murafa.md`, pogroms, named victims, trade, and population counts belong to the relevant settlement history. The Stara/Nova Murafa Judaic registrars own their documented institutional and registration aspects. Identify which settlement or institution a source actually describes; do not assign earlier events to later communities, or equate proprietary halves with registrar jurisdictions, without evidence.
- In `settlement/Politanky.md`, village administration, elections, landholding, and inhabitants remain settlement history. `registrar/Politanky-civil.md`, despite its village-council title, is the home for registration and record-finding context; `registrar/Politanky-orthodox-Michael-miracle.md` covers the church, parish, clergy, and religious registers. A parish source's village population figures do not become exclusively parish history.
- `organization/Sharhorod-orthodox-monastery.md` is justified by the monastery as a distinct institution, with its brotherhood, property, schools, and institutional history—not by the length of the Sharhorod article. Keep meaningful city milestones and links in Sharhorod; distinguish the Basilian and Orthodox phases and associated schools without assuming their institutional equivalence.
