# Research sourced local-history facts

Help the user discover interesting facts without turning plausible stories, search snippets, or weak citations into asserted history. “Proved” means supported by evidence that readers can inspect; do not imply absolute certainty where the sources do not provide it.

This evidence workflow governs claims found in outside sources and the assistant's own inferences. Treat facts the user states—including their own source readings or transcriptions—as established unless they mark uncertainty or explicitly ask for verification. Do not demand corroboration or weaken those facts because outside evidence is absent; if inspected sources conflict, report the difference without silently replacing the user's account.

## Workflow

1. **Define the question.** Identify the place, period, people or institution, and what would count as an interesting answer. Ask a concise question if the scope is too broad to research responsibly.
2. **Check the task queue.** Read relevant notes in `tasks/` before starting. Do not duplicate an open task; if the request spans independently answerable questions, keep the current answer focused and capture the other questions as separate tasks.
3. **Check reusable sources before web searching.** Look through `sources/` for source records relevant to the question, then check the corresponding local copies under `books/`. A source record is a lead and retrieval aid, not evidence; verify the source itself and its relevant passage. A previously downloaded copy may satisfy the requirement to process a source document, but record what you actually inspected and its precise locator. Do not download another copy when a usable local copy is already present.
4. **Search the web and inspect documents.** Every research session must include at least one web search and processing of at least one source document (for example, a page of a book, archival image, map, official text, or a relevant local document). For research concerning the territory of former Sharhorod raion, always search in Ukrainian, then route additional languages by the historical period and subject:
   - **Russian:** research about periods before 1991, and any topic concerning Old Believers.
   - **Polish:** research about periods before 1930, whenever Polish people, institutions, or topics are relevant, and for every Roman Catholic topic regardless of period.
   - **Hebrew and Yiddish:** research concerning Jews or Judaism, and any topic concerning urban life. Search in both languages when this rule applies.
   - **German:** research about periods 1941–1944, and any topic concerning Austrian subjects or Lutheranism.
   - **Romanian:** research about the period 1941–1944.

   This language list is not exhaustive. Infer additional search languages from explicit context such as a person's stated citizenship or origin, an institution, or a relevant source tradition. You may infer a person's origin from their name alone. These rules determine search coverage, not which credible, locally relevant findings may be used.

   Apply period cutoffs to the period being researched, not to the publication date of a source. Adapt each query to the language rather than translating literally; use historically appropriate place names, spellings, administrative terms, and relevant scripts (including Cyrillic and Latin forms, and Hebrew script where appropriate). Do not guess spellings or treat a translated name as the only form. Record the exact queries, the language and reason for using each, and useful results in the task or returned research record. A search result is a lead, not a processed document. Record each inspected document with its title/creator, date, exact page/folio/image or other locator, URL or shelfmark, and what it can establish. Prefer primary sources for claims about events, with reputable secondary sources for context. A catalog description establishes what the catalog says, not the uninspected file's contents.

5. **Use a bounded access workflow.** For each relevant blocked resource, try no more than two materially different automated retrieval methods; do not repeat the same failing request or keep probing a site. Stop immediately at login walls, CAPTCHAs, paywalls, or explicit access restrictions—do not bypass them. Put temporary downloads and command output in ignored `.agent-work/`; put an intentionally retained source copy in ignored `books/` and add or update a reusable record in `sources/` when it is likely to help future research. Record meaningful access failures briefly in the task, not in article prose.
6. **Escalate selectively to the user.** Ask for human retrieval only when a blocked source is important to answering the question (for example, a decisive primary source) and available alternatives are inadequate. Batch essential blocked sources into one request; specify the source, why it matters, the relevant pages/folios or excerpt needed, and ask that a digital copy be placed in `books/` when appropriate. Do not ask for every blocked link or a full large work when only a few pages are needed. If the source is not essential, continue with available evidence and state the limitation. While an essential source or access path is pending, set the task to `blocked`; resume as `in_progress` when it becomes available. Do not use `no_result` while an essential source is pending.
7. **Save worthwhile follow-up leads.** If research uncovers a distinct, potentially useful question that is not answered in the current scope, create a Markdown task in `tasks/` using `tasks/TEMPLATE.md`. Check for duplicates first. Create one note per independently answerable question; do not create an umbrella task or combine unrelated questions. Include the lead/evidence that prompted it, a focused question, useful search terms or source leads, and a completion condition. Capture every material follow-up lead; do not turn trivial tangents into queue noise. One task may target multiple article paths: use the `targets` YAML list for every affected article. Article count does not determine task scope.
8. **Inspect and locate the evidence.** Verify the relevant passage, page, image, folio, entry, or map location in the underlying source where possible. Preserve original spelling in research records outside `content/` when needed to identify or verify evidence. In `content/`, translate or summarize quotations and source wording in Ukrainian; preserve another language only for the exact title of an actual publication. Render foreign personal and place names in Ukrainian script, using established Ukrainian forms or consistent transliteration/transcription without adding the original spelling alongside them. If web search or document access is unavailable, record that in the task or returned research record; do not imply that the minimum was met or put the access failure into article prose.
9. **Be explicit about limits in the right place.** In article prose, include only a concise qualification needed to accurately interpret the historical claim (for example, that a catalog entry establishes what the catalog says, not the contents of an uninspected file). Do not narrate the research process or the agent's environment in an article: failed access attempts, HTTP/tool errors, unavailable tools, and similar operational details belong in the task's research log and evidence/answer sections. Never mark incomplete evidence as `found` or `done`. Do not turn an access failure into article prose merely to disclose that the research was incomplete.
10. **Build a small evidence record.** For each candidate fact, capture:
    - the proposed claim;
    - the evidence and a precise locator;
    - source type (primary/secondary) and what it can establish;
    - caveats, conflicting evidence, and confidence.

    Prefer well-supported, locally relevant findings from research, but do not treat named people as trivia or require fame/notability. Preserve each person's source-supported local role, action, or appearance in the appropriate article, including one-time and otherwise obscure mentions. Seek independent corroboration for consequential or surprising research findings when feasible; do not apply this as a requirement to user-provided facts.

11. **Apply a reader-value gate without discarding people.** For ordinary findings, name the reader-facing question a detail answers and keep search exclusions, low-value negatives, and research-process narration in the task. A named person in a source relevant to the target territory does not need to be famous or independently notable to merit inclusion: preserve their source-supported name and local connection in the appropriate article, even for a single appearance. A role seated in a target settlement can support a qualified inference of probable presence there (for example, a volost chief appointed to a seat in Lozova); explain the evidence and label the inference rather than presenting it as an explicit visit. A person whose only known role is external (for example, a judge in Mogilev) is not locally relevant on that basis alone; include them only when their specific action materially documents the target territory or its inhabitants. Do not add a sentence merely to document that a place, person, or event is absent from an unrelated list.
12. **Treat negative evidence narrowly.** An omission can establish that a name does not appear in a particular inspected list; it does not by itself establish that an event did not happen, that an entity remained somewhere, or that its status and boundaries were unchanged. Before using an omission as evidence, verify that the source is an exhaustive list for the specific action, place, period, and administrative level; that the entity's identity and relevant name/status are stable and match the list; and that plausible renaming, regrouping, or other explanations do not defeat the inference. If those conditions are uncertain, report only the source-bounded observation (if it is reader-useful) and keep the inference in the task. Even a valid negative inference belongs in an article only when it materially answers that article's question; evidential validity alone is not editorial relevance.
13. **Write for `content/` in Ukrainian.** Follow the repository's language rule without exception for requests to write in another language: all authored text, including descriptions and headings, must be Ukrainian. The only other-language text allowed is the exact title of an actual publication. Translate or summarize quotations and other source wording; render foreign personal and place names in Ukrainian script. Follow nearby notes' organization and inline Markdown citation style, put citations next to the claims they support, separate source wording from editorial interpretation, and do not embellish a claim for dramatic effect. Preserve existing frontmatter structure, wikilinks, embeds, and source links.
14. **Apply task findings to their targets.** For a research task, save verified findings and source details in the task first and set `status: found`. Then assess each target article separately: apply relevant, supported findings, preserving the note's structure, citations, and uncertainty; add Obsidian wikilinks to relevant existing notes rather than leaving cross-linking to the user. Preserve source-supported people and their local roles/actions/appearances; do not omit them through the ordinary reader-value gate. For other findings, record in the task why supported details were not included when they fail that gate. When drafting or revising article prose, bold the article's title at its natural first mention. Set `status: done` after all supported local-person mentions and other article-worthy findings are applied and the task records the disposition of remaining findings. If bounded research yields no new supported information or useful inference and no essential source remains pending, record the attempts and limits, leave target articles unchanged unless a supported qualification or local-person mention is warranted, and set `status: no_result`. Do not use `no_result` for interrupted work or to imply the fact/document does not exist; use `queued`, `in_progress`, or `blocked` as appropriate.
    For a standalone request to find facts, do not edit article files unless asked: return the evidence record and suggested Ukrainian wording. State what remains unverified and which sources you actually inspected.

## Atomic task convention

- A task is one answerable research question with one completion outcome. It may require several sources and may target multiple articles, but it is not a list of independent questions or edits.
- Use `targets` as a YAML sequence of article paths (not a singular `target` field). List every article the findings are intended to inform; use an empty sequence only when no article is in scope.
- Keep task notes in `tasks/`, based on `tasks/TEMPLATE.md`. Task states are: `queued` before work begins; `in_progress` while research is underway and can proceed; `blocked` while a specific essential source/access/response is unavailable; `found` when new supported findings and their evidence are saved in the task but article-worthy findings are not yet applied; `done` after article-worthy findings are applied and the task records why other supported findings were kept out of public prose; and `no_result` when bounded research found no useful supported information after alternatives were exhausted and no essential source remains pending. `no_result` is not evidence that the searched-for fact or document does not exist. Resume blocked tasks as `in_progress` when the dependency is met; new leads can also reopen `no_result` tasks as `in_progress`.
- Record web queries and the actual source documents processed in the task. For an ordinary research request without an existing task, include these in the returned evidence record; create task notes only for distinct worthwhile follow-ups.
- Reuse local source copies when available. For blocked sources, record meaningful attempts and make at most one batched human retrieval request when the source is essential; do not let nonessential access failures stall the research.
- When applying findings, add Obsidian wikilinks to relevant existing notes and bold the target article's title at its natural first mention. Do not create links to nonexistent notes or add irrelevant links merely to increase link count.

## Completion check

- Each historical or genealogical assertion derived from research has a reference that supports that specific assertion. User-provided facts do not require independent corroboration; retain any uncertainty the user marked.
- Each research-derived article finding is locally relevant and supported. User-provided facts are treated as established under the rule above. Named people with a source-supported connection to the target territory are not excluded as low-value or insufficiently notable; external people without such a connection are not made local by their name alone.
- Negative evidence is stated only at the scope the source supports; absence from a list is not turned into proof of non-occurrence, continued status, or unchanged boundaries without an exhaustive, identity-matched comparison and consideration of alternatives.
- The locator allows a reader to find the evidence. Article prose distinguishes what a source establishes from what remains uncertain, without exposing research-process or agent-environment failures; those details are recorded in the task.
- The research record includes at least one web search and at least one processed source document, or explicitly records why the work remains incomplete. A source record alone is not a processed document.
- A task marked `found` has evidence recorded but supported local-person mentions and other article-worthy findings have not yet been applied; a task marked `done` has those mentions and findings applied and records why other supported findings failing the reader-value gate were kept out of public prose; a task marked `blocked` names the essential dependency and why it matters; a task marked `no_result` records the bounded search, limits, and why no supported finding or useful inference emerged without implying nonexistence.
- Target article prose added or revised from findings includes appropriate Obsidian wikilinks to relevant existing notes and bolds the article title at its natural first mention.
- Before applying findings, check all new or revised reader-facing text in `content/` for Ukrainian wording and Ukrainian-script rendering of foreign personal and place names; retain another language only in exact publication titles.
- Each worthwhile unanswered follow-up discovered during research has a non-duplicative, atomic task note.
- Names, dates, places, and quoted wording in research records have not been silently normalized or inferred; reader-facing text in `content/` follows the Ukrainian-language and name-rendering rules above.
- The Ukrainian prose does not state more than the evidence supports.
