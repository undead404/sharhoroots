# Research sourced local-history facts

Help the user discover interesting facts without turning plausible stories, search snippets, or weak citations into asserted history. “Proved” means supported by evidence that readers can inspect; do not imply absolute certainty where the sources do not provide it.

## Workflow

1. **Define the question.** Identify the place, period, people or institution, and what would count as an interesting answer. Ask a concise question if the scope is too broad to research responsibly.
2. **Check the task queue.** Read relevant notes in `tasks/` before starting. Do not duplicate an open task; if the request spans independently answerable questions, keep the current answer focused and capture the other questions as separate tasks.
3. **Check reusable sources before web searching.** Look through `sources/` for source records relevant to the question, then check the corresponding local copies under `books/`. A source record is a lead and retrieval aid, not evidence; verify the source itself and its relevant passage. A previously downloaded copy may satisfy the requirement to process a source document, but record what you actually inspected and its precise locator. Do not download another copy when a usable local copy is already present.
4. **Search the web and inspect documents.** Every research session must include at least one web search and processing of at least one source document (for example, a page of a book, archival image, map, official text, or a relevant local document). For Sharhorod-region research, search in Ukrainian, Russian, and Polish when each language could surface relevant sources; do not assume a Ukrainian-only search is sufficient. Adapt queries to each language rather than translating literally: try historically appropriate place names, spellings and administrative terms, including Cyrillic and Latin-script forms where relevant. Add other languages when the place, period, source collections, or people make them useful. Record the exact queries and useful results in the task or returned research record. A search result is a lead, not a processed document. Record each inspected document with its title/creator, date, exact page/folio/image or other locator, URL or shelfmark, and what it can establish. Prefer primary sources for claims about events, with reputable secondary sources for context. A catalog description establishes what the catalog says, not the uninspected file's contents.
5. **Use a bounded access workflow.** For each relevant blocked resource, try no more than two materially different automated retrieval methods; do not repeat the same failing request or keep probing a site. Stop immediately at login walls, CAPTCHAs, paywalls, or explicit access restrictions—do not bypass them. Put temporary downloads and command output in ignored `.agent-work/`; put an intentionally retained source copy in ignored `books/` and add or update a reusable record in `sources/` when it is likely to help future research. Record meaningful access failures briefly in the task, not in article prose.
6. **Escalate selectively to the user.** Ask for human retrieval only when a blocked source is important to answering the question (for example, a decisive primary source) and available alternatives are inadequate. Batch essential blocked sources into one request; specify the source, why it matters, the relevant pages/folios or excerpt needed, and ask that a digital copy be placed in `books/` when appropriate. Do not ask for every blocked link or a full large work when only a few pages are needed. If the source is not essential, continue with available evidence and state the limitation. While an essential user-provided source is pending, leave the task `open` and do not mark it `fail`; mark `fail` only after the source is unavailable/declined or alternatives are exhausted and no new supported information or useful inference can be added.
7. **Save worthwhile follow-up leads.** If research uncovers a distinct, potentially useful question that is not answered in the current scope, create a Markdown task in `tasks/` using `tasks/TEMPLATE.md`. Check for duplicates first. Create one note per independently answerable question; do not create an umbrella task or combine unrelated questions. Include the lead/evidence that prompted it, a focused question, useful search terms or source leads, and a completion condition. Capture every material follow-up lead; do not turn trivial tangents into queue noise. One task may target multiple article paths: use the `targets` YAML list for every affected article. Article count does not determine task scope.
8. **Inspect and locate the evidence.** Verify the relevant passage, page, image, folio, entry, or map location in the underlying source where possible. Preserve original spelling in quotations; translate or summarize separately. If web search or document access is unavailable, record that in the task or returned research record; do not imply that the minimum was met or put the access failure into article prose.
9. **Be explicit about limits in the right place.** In article prose, include only a concise qualification needed to accurately interpret the historical claim (for example, that a catalog entry establishes what the catalog says, not the contents of an uninspected file). Do not narrate the research process or the agent's environment in an article: failed access attempts, HTTP/tool errors, unavailable tools, and similar operational details belong in the task's research log and evidence/answer sections. Never mark incomplete evidence as `found` or `done`. Do not turn an access failure into article prose merely to disclose that the research was incomplete.
10. **Build a small evidence record.** For each candidate fact, capture:
   - the proposed claim;
   - the evidence and a precise locator;
   - source type (primary/secondary) and what it can establish;
   - caveats, conflicting evidence, and confidence.

   Prefer a smaller number of well-supported, locally relevant facts over a long list of trivia. Seek independent corroboration for consequential or surprising claims when feasible.

11. **Write in Ukrainian.** When asked to draft or add prose, follow nearby notes' organization and inline Markdown citation style. Put citations next to the claims they support. Separate source wording from editorial interpretation; do not embellish a claim for dramatic effect. Preserve existing frontmatter, wikilinks, embeds, and source links.
12. **Apply task findings to their targets.** For a research task, save verified findings and source details in the task first and set `status: found`. Then apply relevant, supported findings to every applicable article in `targets`, preserving the note's structure, citations, and uncertainty; add Obsidian wikilinks to relevant existing notes rather than leaving cross-linking to the user. When drafting or revising article prose, bold the article's title at its natural first mention. Set `status: done` only after the target article changes are applied. If no new supported information can be added and no essential source is pending from the user, record what was attempted and why, leave target articles unchanged, and set `status: fail`. Do not mark an interrupted, not-yet-researched, or awaiting-source task `fail`; leave it `open`.
   For a standalone request to find facts, do not edit article files unless asked: return the evidence record and suggested Ukrainian wording. State what remains unverified and which sources you actually inspected.

## Atomic task convention

- A task is one answerable research question with one completion outcome. It may require several sources and may target multiple articles, but it is not a list of independent questions or edits.
- Use `targets` as a YAML sequence of article paths (not a singular `target` field). List every article the findings are intended to inform; use an empty sequence only when no article is in scope.
- Keep task notes in `tasks/`, based on `tasks/TEMPLATE.md`. Task states are: `open` while work is pending or incomplete (including while an essential source is awaited from the user); `found` when new supported findings and their evidence are saved in the task but have not yet been applied to target articles; `done` only after those findings are applied to the relevant target articles; and `fail` when no new supported information can be added after alternatives are exhausted and no essential source remains pending, with the reason and attempts recorded. Do not change targets for a `fail` task.
- Record web queries and the actual source documents processed in the task. For an ordinary research request without an existing task, include these in the returned evidence record; create task notes only for distinct worthwhile follow-ups.
- Reuse local source copies when available. For blocked sources, record meaningful attempts and make at most one batched human retrieval request when the source is essential; do not let nonessential access failures stall the research.
- When applying findings, add Obsidian wikilinks to relevant existing notes and bold the target article's title at its natural first mention. Do not create links to nonexistent notes or add irrelevant links merely to increase link count.

## Completion check

- Each historical or genealogical assertion in proposed prose has a reference that supports that specific assertion.
- The locator allows a reader to find the evidence. Article prose distinguishes what a source establishes from what remains uncertain, without exposing research-process or agent-environment failures; those details are recorded in the task.
- The research record includes at least one web search and at least one processed source document, or explicitly records why the work remains incomplete. A source record alone is not a processed document.
- A task marked `found` has evidence recorded but its target articles have not yet been updated; a task marked `done` has its applicable target articles updated; a task marked `fail` records why no new supported information could be added and leaves targets unchanged.
- Target article prose added or revised from findings includes appropriate Obsidian wikilinks to relevant existing notes and bolds the article title at its natural first mention.
- Each worthwhile unanswered follow-up discovered during research has a non-duplicative, atomic task note.
- Names, dates, places, and quoted wording have not been silently normalized or inferred.
- The Ukrainian prose does not state more than the evidence supports.
